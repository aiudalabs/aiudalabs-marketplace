# Competitor Analysis Framework

Competitive analysis in positioning is not about who to attack — it's about understanding the full landscape of alternatives a buyer considers, including doing nothing. Use this framework to map the competitive environment and identify where the startup's message is vulnerable.

---

## 1. Competitor categories

### Direct competitors
Companies offering the same service to the same ICP in the same category.
- For a B2B AI implementation agency in LATAM: Other AI consulting firms targeting mid-market Latin American companies
- Identify: What do they charge? How do they position? What's their proof?

### Indirect competitors
Different category, same budget, same problem.
- For AI implementation: Management consulting firms with a "digital transformation" practice
- Big 4 consulting firms offering AI advisory at enterprise level
- Cloud providers (AWS, Google, Azure) offering "AI adoption" programs

### Status quo / do nothing
The buyer continues doing what they're doing now. Often the #1 competitor.
- Manual Excel-based processes
- "We'll figure it out in-house"
- "We'll wait until AI matures more"
- "We tried and it was too complicated"

### Internal team build
"Our IT team can do this."
- Especially common in larger companies with a technical team
- Risk: They often can't, but the belief that they can delays buying

### Freelancer/agency alternatives
Individual AI freelancers on Fiverr/Upwork or small agencies without a structured methodology.
- Usually cheaper, less reliable, less accountable
- Buyers often try this first and come back for proper implementation

### Generic AI tools
"We'll just use ChatGPT/Copilot/whatever ourselves."
- Very common buyer reflex
- Often fails because there's no process design, training, or maintenance
- Creates a warm market: buyers who tried and failed come back for professional help

**What to look for:**
- Where are all competitors saying the same thing? (This is where the white space is)
- Where is the startup saying the exact same thing as competitors? (This is a vulnerability)
- Which competitors have stronger proof? (This is a credibility gap to close)

## 3. Differentiation map

Map all competitors on two dimensions:

**X-axis:** Generalist → Specialist
**Y-axis:** Self-serve / low-touch → High-touch / advisory

Position the startup and competitors on this map. Look for:
- Uncrowded quadrant where the startup could own a position
- Crowded quadrant where the startup will struggle to differentiate
- Quadrant mismatch: Where the startup's positioning doesn't match where they actually sit

**Common patterns for B2B AI companies:**
- Most competitors are generic ("AI for any business")
- White space is usually in vertical specialization + advisory/implementation (not just software)
- LATAM-specific positioning is almost always a white space

## 4. Message comparison analysis

For each direct competitor, analyze their homepage against this checklist:

**What are they claiming?**
- Category claim
- Primary differentiation language
- Proof they use
- CTA type
- Who they name as ICP

**What are they NOT claiming?** (This is where differentiation opportunities live)
- Missing geography specificity
- Missing vertical specificity
- Missing methodology claim
- Missing maintenance/post-delivery support claim
- Missing ROI specificity

**Questions to surface:**
- Is the startup's differentiation actually visible in their copy?
- Or does their copy sound identical to the competitor?
- What's one thing the startup does that no competitor claims? This should be in the headline.

## 5. Pricing landscape

Map what is known or can be inferred about competitor pricing:

| Competitor | Pricing model | Price range (est.) | Pricing visible on site? |
|------------|---------------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| | | | |

**Common pricing models for B2B AI services:**
- Project-based: $5K–$150K depending on scope
- Monthly retainer: $2K–$20K/month
- Training: $500–$5K per day/cohort
- Productized package: $1K–$25K fixed
- Free tier / freemium: Usually for software tools, not services

**Evaluation questions:**
- Does the startup's pricing model fit the market?
- Is their pricing positioned at the right tier given their proof and credibility?
- Are they priced like a junior freelancer when they want to be perceived as a premium firm?
- Are they priced like an enterprise firm when their proof only supports SME-level engagements?
