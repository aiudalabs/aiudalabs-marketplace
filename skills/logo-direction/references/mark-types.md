# Types of mark

## The options

| Type | What it is | Works well when | Weak when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wordmark | The name, set in distinctive type | The name is short and distinctive; the company is new and needs the name learned | The name is long, or must fit a square space |
| Lettermark | Initials or a monogram | The name is long; a compact mark is needed | The initials mean nothing yet to the audience |
| Symbol | A pictorial or abstract mark with no text | The brand is already known, or always appears beside its name | The company is new: nobody knows what the symbol stands for |
| Combination | Symbol and wordmark together, each usable alone | Most new companies: the name is learned and the symbol covers small spaces | Neither part is strong enough alone |
| Emblem | Name inside a shape, as one unit | Tradition or craft is the point; badges, labels, seals | Small sizes: the text inside becomes unreadable |

## How to choose

Decide from constraints, not from taste.

1. **Where is the smallest place it appears?** A browser tab or an app icon needs something that works in a small square. A wordmark alone will not. Plan a symbol or a lettermark for that space.
2. **How long is the name?** Up to about eight characters, a wordmark can carry the brand. Longer names usually need a compact companion.
3. **How known is the company?** A new company needs its name read. A symbol alone hides it.
4. **What do competitors use?** If every competitor has an abstract symbol beside a sans-serif name, the same structure will blend in. Check the follow-or-break decision in the brief.
5. **Who will produce it?** Wordmarks and simple geometric symbols can be built precisely from basic shapes. Illustrative marks, mascots and custom lettering need a designer.

For most new companies the practical answer is a combination mark: a wordmark for the name, plus a simple symbol or lettermark derived from it for small spaces.

## What makes a mark work

- **One idea.** A mark that tries to say three things says none.
- **Simple enough to redraw from memory.** If a person cannot sketch it roughly after seeing it once, it is too complex.
- **Distinct in silhouette.** Filled in solid black, it should still be itself.
- **Independent of color.** It must work in one color. Color is added to a mark that already works.
- **Stable when small.** Thin lines, small gaps and fine detail disappear at small sizes.
- **Free of effects.** Gradients, shadows and bevels date quickly and fail in one-color uses.

## Responsive logos

A logo is usually a small family, not one file:

| Space available | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Wide | Full combination mark, horizontal |
| Square or narrow | Stacked version |
| Small | Symbol or lettermark alone |
| Very small, such as a browser tab | A simplified symbol with fine detail removed |

Design the smallest version deliberately. Scaling the full logo down is not a small version.
