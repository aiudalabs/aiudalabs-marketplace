# Starting from zero

Most identities are built for a company that has nothing yet. There is no page to evolve and no equity to protect, so the look has to come from somewhere else. It comes from taste, and taste has to be collected before it can be used.

## Collect taste before designing

A brief describes what the brand should mean. It does not say what the owner finds attractive. Ask for that directly:

1. **Three brands or sites whose look you like, from any industry.** For each, what is it you like?
2. **One you dislike**, and why.
3. **Three words for how it should feel to land on your site.**

Look at each reference yourself. Screenshot it and write down what it actually does: how it uses color, what its type is like, whether it is calm or loud, flat or layered. People often say "clean" about pages that are in fact bold and high-contrast, so trust the reference over the adjective.

If the owner has no references, show them things to react to. A quick board of six to eight very different real sites, with the question "which two feel closest to what you want?", gets better information than an interview.

If the owner cannot be asked at all, say so, pick references from the audience's world (what they read, the tools they use, the brands they already trust), record that the references were your choice, and treat every later decision as provisional.

## Turn taste into directions

References are for extracting qualities, never for copying. From what the owner likes, name the qualities:

- "heavy headline type with a lot of confidence"
- "one saturated color against a soft background"
- "a dark object on a light page"
- "feels made by people, not by a committee"

Then build each direction around a different combination of those qualities, plus one idea of your own that the references did not contain. A direction that only recombines references is a pastiche.

## Where the character comes from

With no existing identity, each direction needs a source for its character. Choose it on purpose:

| Source | Example |
| --- | --- |
| The company's own activity | A workshop company whose pages look like a working session: marked-up, in progress |
| Its audience's world | Operations people: schedules, boards, dashboards made beautiful |
| A material or craft | Print, signage, blueprints, packaging, notebooks |
| A strong type voice | One typeface with a real personality, used large |
| A color with nerve | An unusual pairing owned completely |
| A shape or motif | One recurring form that carries from logo to layout |

Pick a different source for each direction. That is the fastest way to make them differ.

## The bar

Without a current identity to compare against, compare against the best-looking sites the buyer will also see: the strongest competitor, and the owner's own references. Put them on the board as references. The question for each direction is whether it holds up beside them.

A from-zero direction is finished when:

- it has warmth, contrast and one thing to remember
- it would not be mistaken for a template
- you would choose it over the strongest competitor's page

If it does not get there, keep working on it or drop it. Do not present it with caveats.

## What not to do

- Do not derive the look from attributes by logic. Attributes guide; they do not generate. A palette deduced from three adjectives is how correct, lifeless identities are made.
- Do not let differentiation choose for you. Avoiding everything competitors do removes options that may suit the brand best.
- Do not settle the parts one at a time. Color, type and logo are judged together, on a page.
- Do not treat plainness as honesty. Restraint is a style that needs as much craft as exuberance, and more nerve.
