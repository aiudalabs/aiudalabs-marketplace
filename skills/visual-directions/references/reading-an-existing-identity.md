# Reading an existing identity

Before changing a brand, find out what makes it itself. Most identities that feel right owe it to three or four specific moves. Lose those and you have replaced the brand, even if every new value is correct.

## Capture it

- Screenshot the home page at desktop width, top to bottom, and one inner page.
- Read the stylesheet or theme file for the real values: colors, font families, sizes, radii, shadows.
- Collect the logo files.

## Find the character

Go through the page and name what you would miss if it were removed.

| Look at | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| The strongest color moment | Where does color appear, and how much? One button, or a whole section? |
| Type contrast | Is there a pairing that creates tension: heavy with light, sans with an italic serif, large with tiny? |
| The signature detail | Is there one recurring move: a label style, a bracket, an underline, a shape? |
| Surfaces | Flat or layered? Sharp or rounded? Are there shadows, glows, textures, gradients? |
| Light and dark | Is there a dark element on a light page, or the reverse? Contrast between sections gives rhythm. |
| The logo | What idea does it carry, and how does the rest of the page echo it? |
| Density | Airy or packed? |
| Motion | Does anything move, and does it matter? |

Write the equity as a short list of concrete moves, such as:

- "Very heavy sans headline with one phrase in an italic serif"
- "Orange used only for actions and one accent phrase, on a cream background"
- "One dark, rounded panel in the hero against the light page"

That list is what direction one must keep.

## Find what is wrong

Separate two kinds of problem.

**Measurable.** These get fixed, in any direction:

- text and background pairs that fail contrast
- values that drift: five slightly different greys, three corner radii with no rule
- a logo that depends on an installed font, or blurs when small
- fonts with an unclear license

When repairing, change as little as possible. A muted grey that fails contrast becomes the nearest grey that passes, in the same hue. It does not become a new palette.

**Judgment.** These are opinions and belong in a direction, where they can be seen and accepted or rejected:

- "the hero says three things at once"
- "the accent is used too often to mean anything"
- "it looks like the rest of its category"

## Ask the owner

If the owner is available, two questions are worth more than any audit:

1. What do you like most about how it looks now?
2. What bothers you about it?

Their answers outrank your reading. If the owner likes something you would change, it stays in direction one.

## A warning about differentiation

Finding that competitors share a trait with the brand is information, not a verdict. A warm accent that suits the brand is not wrong because others in the category also use warm accents. Being different matters only when the brand is actually being confused with someone else, or when the shared trait is the weakest part of the identity. A distinctive look that the owner dislikes has failed.
