# Offer Design Framework

The offer is what a buyer actually commits to — not the category, not the vision, not the technology. If the offer is unclear, mismatched, or missing, the best positioning in the world won't generate revenue. This framework evaluates what the company is actually selling and whether it's set up to convert.

---

## 1. Wedge offer

**What it is:** A low-commitment, high-value entry point that gets a buyer into the relationship without requiring them to trust you with a large budget first. The best wedge offers deliver a clear outcome in a short time frame.

**Evaluation questions:**
- Is there an entry-point offer on the homepage that a skeptical buyer could say yes to without a long procurement process?
- Does the wedge offer deliver a real outcome (not just "a conversation")?
- Is the wedge offer priced (or free) in a way that matches the buyer's risk tolerance?
- Does the wedge offer create natural expansion into a larger engagement?

**Strong wedge offers for AI services companies:**
- "Free AI Readiness Audit" (1–2 hour discovery, produces a report)
- "1-week AI process mapping sprint" (fixed price, clear deliverable)
- "1 AI agent proof-of-concept, built in 5 days" (tangible, low-risk demo)

**Weak wedge offers:**
- "Book a call" — not an offer, it's a step
- "Get a quote" — buyer has no idea what they're evaluating
- "Schedule a demo" — fine for software, weak for services

---

## 2. Diagnostic offer

**What it is:** A structured first engagement where the company provides a specific, valuable analysis or assessment before any implementation begins. Diagnostics convert well because they give the buyer something concrete and position the seller as an expert.

**What good looks like:**
- "AI Process Audit: We analyze your 3 highest-volume workflows and identify the top 2 automation opportunities. Delivered in 5 business days. Fixed fee: $X."
- Deliverable is a written report or presentation — something tangible
- The diagnosis creates natural urgency to act on its findings
- Buyer understands exactly what they get and what it costs

**Common failures:**
- Diagnostic described as "discovery call" — no tangible deliverable
- Diagnostic priced too high for early-stage trust ($5K+ audit for a cold buyer won't convert)
- Diagnostic outcome is a sales pitch, not genuine advice

---

## 3. Sprint offer

**What it is:** A time-boxed, fixed-scope engagement with a defined deliverable. Works well for AI implementation because it limits buyer risk and forces delivery clarity.

**What good looks like:**
- "4-week AI Implementation Sprint: We design, build, and deploy 1 AI agent in your workflow. Your team is trained and can maintain it independently. Price: $X."
- Time is fixed (2–8 weeks)
- Deliverables are specific and named
- Success criteria are stated
- Clear handoff at the end

**Questions to ask:**
- Is there a sprint offer (or similar) visible on the homepage?
- Are deliverables named, or is it described as "custom work tailored to your needs"?
- Is the time frame stated?
- Is the price or price range visible?

---

## 4. Implementation offer

**What it is:** A larger, multi-phase engagement for companies ready to commit to systematic AI adoption. Usually follows a sprint or diagnostic.

**Questions to evaluate:**
- Is there an implementation offer (larger engagement) described anywhere?
- Is it clear how a buyer progresses from wedge offer to full implementation?
- Are milestones or phases described?
- Is the team involved in delivery described?

**Note for evaluation:** If an implementation offer is the only offer visible, this is a red flag — only warm buyers or inbound referrals will convert. Cold buyers need a lower-commitment entry point first.

---

## 5. Training offer

**What it is:** Structured learning programs for teams — AI literacy, tool-specific training, or ongoing capability building. High relevance for LATAM market where AI adoption readiness varies widely.

**Questions to evaluate:**
- Is training offered as a standalone product?
- Is training described with specific curriculum, format, and duration?
- Is training positioned as an entry point or an upsell?
- Does training connect to implementation (i.e., does it create natural demand for implementation services)?

**Strong training offers for B2B AI companies:**
- "AI for Operations Teams: 1-day workshop on automation fundamentals + hands-on with 2 tools"
- "AI Champion Program: 6-week cohort to train internal advocates who lead adoption"

---

## 6. Productized service

**What it is:** A service packaged with a fixed scope, fixed price, and defined deliverable — treated like a product, not a custom engagement. Productization makes services easier to buy, compare, and scale.

**Questions to evaluate:**
- Is any service packaged as a productized offering with a fixed price and defined scope?
- Does the packaging make the service easy to buy without a long discovery process?
- Is there a clear service name (not just "Implementation Services")?
- Is pricing visible or at least a price signal (starting at $X, typical range $X–$Y)?

**Examples of productized AI services:**
- "AI Starter Pack: 3 AI agent automations deployed in 6 weeks. $X."
- "WhatsApp AI Bot for Customer Service: Built, deployed, and trained in 3 weeks. $X."
- "Data Analysis Copilot: Weekly business intelligence report generated by AI. $X/month."

---

## 7. Pricing signal

**What it tests:** Does the buyer have any sense of what this costs before they get on a call?

**Why it matters:** Buyers who don't know the price range will not convert on a discovery call unless the company is exceptional. Pricing signals filter out non-buyers before the call and build trust with qualified buyers.

**Evaluation:**
- `[GOOD]` — Price ranges visible ("Starting at $3,000"), or pricing tiers, or clear signals ("Enterprise only" vs. "For teams of 50+")
- `[ACCEPTABLE]` — No price listed but clear ROI framing makes the case ("Clients typically save $X/month")
- `[WEAK]` — Completely opaque — "Contact us for pricing" with no signals
- `[FLAG]` — Pricing absent while the service requires significant buyer trust (consulting, implementation)

---

## 8. Deliverables

**What it tests:** Can the buyer answer "what do I actually get" from reading the homepage?

**Evaluation checklist:**
- [ ] Named deliverables (report, agent, dashboard, training, playbook)
- [ ] Format specified (written, live training, deployed system, async course)
- [ ] Timeline specified (days, weeks, months)
- [ ] Who receives the deliverable (buyer, team, IT department)
- [ ] What the buyer can do with it after delivery

**Common failure:** "We'll build a custom AI solution for your business" — says nothing.

---

## 9. Time-to-value (TTV)

**What it tests:** How quickly does the buyer see real results? Shorter TTV reduces buyer risk and increases willingness to start.

**Questions to ask:**
- What is the TTV of the main offer?
- Is TTV stated in the copy?
- Can the company offer a faster first-value moment (e.g., a diagnostic report in 5 days before a 12-week implementation)?
- Does the TTV match buyer expectations for the price point?

**Benchmark TTV by offer type:**
- Free audit/diagnostic: 1–5 business days
- Sprint/proof of concept: 2–4 weeks
- Full implementation: 8–16 weeks
- Training: 1–3 days per cohort

---

## 10. Risk reversal

**What it tests:** What happens if the buyer is unhappy? Is there any mechanism to reduce buyer risk?

**Risk reversal mechanisms:**
- Money-back guarantee (unusual in services, but powerful if offered)
- Fixed-price guarantee (no scope creep cost)
- Pilot-first model (start small, expand only if satisfied)
- "Train your team to maintain it independently" — reduces vendor lock-in risk
- "We don't lock you in" — explicitly removes a common objection
- Free initial diagnostic with no commitment to continue

**Questions to ask:**
- Is there any risk reversal language in the offer description?
- Does the company address the "what if it doesn't work?" concern explicitly?
- For AI services: Is there a "your team will be able to maintain this after we leave" guarantee? (This is a powerful differentiator for LATAM buyers worried about dependency)
