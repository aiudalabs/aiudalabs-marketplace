# ICP Framework

ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) analysis determines whether the startup knows who it's actually selling to — and whether the website speaks to that person. Vague ICPs create vague messaging. Vague messaging creates zero conversions.

---

## 1. Buyer vs. user vs. influencer

In B2B, the person who pays is often not the person who uses the product, and neither may be the person who finds it.

**Buyer:** Controls the budget and signs the contract. Often CEO, COO, CTO, or department head. Primary concerns: ROI, risk, credibility, integration effort.

**User:** Interacts with the solution daily. Often operations team, data analysts, or department staff. Primary concerns: ease of use, time savings, not breaking existing workflows.

**Influencer:** Recommends, champions, or blocks the decision. Often IT, legal, or a trusted advisor. Primary concerns: security, compliance, vendor reputation.

**Questions to ask about the startup's ICP:**
- Is the homepage copy written for the buyer, user, or influencer?
- Do the pain points named match buyer pains or user pains?
- Does the social proof appeal to the decision-maker's concerns?
- For LATAM companies: Is the cultural communication style appropriate for the decision-maker tier?

**Common failure:** Copy written for users (technical features) when buyers (ROI, risk) are who actually say yes.

---

## 2. Verticals

**What to evaluate:** Is the company targeting all industries ("any business wanting AI") or specific verticals where the pain is acute and the buyer profile is consistent?

**Questions to ask:**
- Which verticals are named explicitly in the copy?
- Are these verticals ones where the pain is genuinely acute and the budget to solve it exists?
- For B2B AI in LATAM: High-signal verticals include logistics, manufacturing, financial services, retail, healthcare administration, and professional services
- Does narrowing to a vertical make the company less competitive (fewer buyers) or more competitive (clearer positioning)?

**Evaluation:**
- `[GOOD]` — One or two named verticals with specific language
- `[WEAK]` — "Any company looking to adopt AI" — targets nobody
- `[RISKY]` — Vertical requires long sales cycles or procurement complexity without addressing this

---

## 3. Company size

**What to evaluate:** Is the target company size stated or implied? Size determines budget, decision speed, and the nature of the problem.

**Size tiers for B2B AI in LATAM:**
- **1–20 employees:** Usually no budget, DIY instinct, need self-serve or very low-cost entry. Wrong fit for $10K+ projects.
- **20–100 employees:** Owner/founder is buyer. Decision fast but budget constrained. Best fit: packaged, fixed-scope offers.
- **100–500 employees:** Mid-market. Department head is buyer. Budget exists. Best fit: pilot projects + expansion.
- **500–5000 employees:** Procurement involved. Longer sales cycle. Budget significant. Best fit: enterprise-grade delivery with security/compliance coverage.
- **5000+ employees:** Enterprise. Not realistic for a new consulting firm without established relationships.

**Questions to ask:**
- What size is implied by the case studies, client logos, or language?
- Does the pricing model (if visible) match the implied company size?
- Does the onboarding model match the decision-making process for that size?

---

## 4. Budget owner

**What to evaluate:** Who controls the money, and does the copy speak to their specific concerns?

**Budget owner types and what they care about:**
- **CEO/Founder:** ROI, competitive advantage, risk of not acting, team capability
- **COO:** Process efficiency, error reduction, integration complexity, team adoption
- **CTO/IT Director:** Security, API contracts, data sovereignty, maintenance burden, team skill requirements
- **CFO:** Cost reduction, payback period, cost of implementation vs. ongoing cost
- **Department Head (Ops/Sales/HR):** Time savings, specific workflow improvement, headcount avoidance

**Common failure:** Copy written in generic "business value" language that doesn't speak to the real concern of the actual budget owner.

---

## 5. Pain intensity

**What to evaluate:** Is the pain being solved urgent (they're bleeding now) or latent (nice to have eventually)?

**Pain intensity spectrum:**
- **Critical/Acute:** The current situation is costing them money, causing errors, or creating competitive disadvantage today. Buyer is actively looking for a solution. High conversion probability.
- **Important/Moderate:** They know they should address this, but it's not on fire. May buy within 6–12 months.
- **Latent:** They don't fully recognize the problem yet. Requires education before selling. Long sales cycles.

**Questions to ask:**
- Is the pain named in the hero section acute or latent?
- Is there language that signals urgency ("while your competitors already…", "every month you wait…")?
- For AI adoption companies: The pain of "not having AI" is currently latent for many LATAM companies. The pain of "having tried ChatGPT with no results" is acute.

---

## 6. Buying triggers

**What to evaluate:** What event would cause this buyer to start searching for a solution today?

**Common B2B AI buying triggers:**
- Competitor announced AI capabilities
- Board/investor pushed digital transformation mandate
- Key employee left and automated workflow is now broken
- Audit/compliance requirement revealed manual process gap
- Failed internal AI project — now want external expertise
- New department head with "AI mandate"
- Cost-cutting initiative driving automation consideration

**Questions to ask:**
- Does the copy acknowledge any of these triggers?
- Is the company positioned to capture a buyer in mid-trigger (actively searching)?
- Is the company positioned to create a trigger for a passive buyer?

---

## 7. Objections

**What to evaluate:** Every B2B buyer has objections. Does the homepage address the most obvious ones?

**Common B2B AI adoption objections in LATAM:**
- "We don't have the internal team to maintain it after you leave."
- "Our data is sensitive — we can't use cloud AI tools."
- "We tried ChatGPT and it didn't work / was too hard for our team."
- "We don't know where to start — AI feels overwhelming."
- "What happens if it breaks after the project ends?"
- "Our IT department will block this."
- "I don't have budget approved for this."
- "We're not sure AI is ready for our industry."
- "How is this different from what [Big Consulting Firm] would offer?"

**Questions to ask:**
- Which of these objections does the homepage address explicitly?
- Are objections addressed proactively in the copy (preemptively) or only in FAQs?
- Which objections are completely unaddressed? Flag each one as a conversion blocker.

---

## 8. Urgency

**What to evaluate:** Is there a reason for the buyer to act this quarter rather than next year?

**Sources of real urgency for B2B AI companies:**
- Competitive pressure ("Your main competitors are already automating this")
- Cost of delay ("Every month of manual processing costs approx. X")
- Market timing ("The AI adoption window for early-mover advantage is 12–18 months")
- Capacity constraint ("We take 3 new clients per quarter")

**False urgency to flag:** `[FLAG: URGENCY CLAIM UNSUPPORTED]`
- "Limited spots available" without any context
- "Offer ends soon" with no date
- "Book now before prices increase" with no evidence

---

## 9. Use cases by ICP

**What to evaluate:** Does the homepage show specific use cases that resonate for each likely ICP?

**Mapping use cases to ICPs (example for B2B AI companies):**

| ICP | Resonant use case | Wrong use case |
|-----|------------------|----------------|
| Logistics/supply chain COO | Automated invoice matching, supplier communication | Internal HR chatbot |
| Mid-market CEO | AI-powered sales pipeline analysis | Technical data pipeline |
| Operations Manager | Automated report generation, exception alerts | Code generation |
| HR Department | Onboarding automation, document processing | Complex ML models |
| Financial Services Compliance | Document review, audit trail automation | Creative content generation |

**Questions to ask:**
- Are use cases specific to an ICP, or generic?
- Do use cases connect to the named pain (not just "AI can also do...") ?
- Are there use cases listed that won't convert for this ICP? (Those are diluting clarity)

---

## 10. Anti-ICP

**What to evaluate:** Does the company know who they DON'T want as a customer? Claiming anti-ICP builds trust by demonstrating focus.

**Anti-ICP signals for B2B AI companies:**
- Companies that want to buy software, not a service
- Startups under 10 employees without budget
- Companies that want a one-day training with no follow-through
- Companies looking for the cheapest possible option (not value-focused)
- Companies that want to own all IP and documentation from day one at no cost

**Questions to ask:**
- Does the copy inadvertently attract the wrong buyer? (e.g., solo entrepreneurs responding to "companies of all sizes")
- Is there any signal that filters out non-ideal customers?
- Would the company benefit from adding qualification signals?
