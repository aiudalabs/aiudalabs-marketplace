# Visual competitor audit

The goal is to learn what the category looks like, so the brand can follow or break its conventions on purpose.

## Who to audit

- Two or three direct competitors: same offer, same buyer
- One or two adjacent brands the buyer already trusts, even from another category
- The category leader, always, because it defines what "normal" looks like

Five is enough. More than that and the pattern gets harder to see, not easier.

## What to look at

Look at the homepage, the product or app if visible, and one social profile. For each competitor record:

| Element | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Primary color | The color that dominates. Give the hex if you can read it from the page, otherwise describe it ("saturated mid blue"). |
| Supporting colors | Accent color and whether neutrals are warm, cool or pure grey |
| Light or dark | Which is the default surface |
| Typeface | Style, not just name: geometric sans, humanist sans, grotesque, serif, slab, mono. One family or two. |
| Logo | Wordmark, lettermark, symbol plus wordmark, emblem. Case. Weight. |
| Shape language | Sharp corners or rounded, dense or airy, flat or with depth |
| Imagery | Product screenshots, photography of people, illustration, abstract 3D, none |
| Tone signalled | Two or three words for what the whole thing communicates |

Record the URL and the date for every observation. Sites change.

## How to read the results

### Conventions

List anything that most of the set shares. Typical findings look like:

- "Four of five use a blue primary on a white surface."
- "All use a geometric sans-serif for headings."
- "All show product screenshots above the fold; none shows people."

### Unclaimed territory

List what nobody does. This is where differentiation is cheapest:

- A hue family nobody uses
- A typeface style nobody uses, such as a serif in a category of sans-serifs
- A surface nobody uses, such as dark by default
- An imagery type nobody uses

Unclaimed is not automatically good. Sometimes nobody does it because buyers in the category would not trust it. That is what the follow-or-break decision in the brief is for.

## Honesty rules

- If you could not load a site, write "not observed" in its row. Do not describe it from memory.
- If a hex value was not read from the page itself, describe the color in words instead of guessing a value.
- Say at the top of the audit whether it was observed or based on the user's description.
